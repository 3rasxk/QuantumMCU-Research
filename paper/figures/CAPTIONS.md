## Fig. 1

Execution time per repetition versus number of qubits n, measured with each board's `micros()` clock, for four circuits
(h_all, bell_ghz, qft, grover) on the Arduino Uno, ESP8266 and ESP32 (one panel per board; log y axis, time in microseconds).
Lines connect the median over the recorded repetitions; vertical bars are the interquartile range (25th to 75th percentile);
faint dots are the individual repetitions. Filled markers: 100 repetitions. Open markers: points stopped by the 2 h per-point
time cap before 100 repetitions (ESP8266 grover n = 9: 37 reps, n = 10: 4 reps; ESP32 grover n = 11: 13 reps, n = 12: 1 rep);
the n = 12 point has a single repetition and therefore no bar. Crosses at the top mark the smallest n at which a memory wall was
recorded (Uno: build overflow, 110 % of SRAM; ESP8266 and ESP32: allocation failure at run time); stars mark points not run
because one repetition was predicted to exceed the time cap ("time-limited, not run"). Line style and marker differ per circuit.

## Fig. 2

Difference between the on-board `micros()` measurement and the pulse width measured by an external ESP32-based counter,
per point (n, circuit) and board: median and interquartile range over the paired repetitions (at least 10 pairs per point),
in microseconds (top) and as a percentage of the counter width (bottom); signed symmetric-log axes, negative means `micros()`
reports less than the counter. Faint dots are the individual pairs; points of one n are shifted horizontally by at most 0.2 for
legibility. Filled markers: the counter reported no anomaly for the point; open markers: the counter flagged an anomaly
(for example extra pulses ignored when pairing by pulse width). The counter was only self-loopback tested and is not
externally calibrated, so all differences are relative to this counter, not to a traceable time reference.
Points with fewer than 10 paired repetitions, or without a joined comparison, are not drawn.

## Fig. 3 (Phase 3, offload)

Round-trip latency per request versus number of qubits n when the board offloads the circuit simulation to a PC over a serial
link (115200 baud), reported by the board's own `micros()` clock; one panel per board, four circuits, log y axis in microseconds.
Lines connect the median over the recorded requests, bars are the interquartile range, faint dots are the individual requests.
Filled markers: 100 requests. Open markers: points stopped by the 2 h per-point time cap before 100 requests
(ESP8266 and ESP32 grover, the last three n each; the last has 2 requests). Stars: points not run because the PC-side simulation
was predicted to exceed the cap or the PC's memory ("PC-simulation-limited, not run"). No external counter reference is used for this
figure; latency includes serial transfer, PC simulation and the reply.
