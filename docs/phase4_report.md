Generated by `analysis/phase4/build_report.py` from `data/processed/phase4/*.csv` and `data/derived/*.csv`, which `analysis/reproduce.py` writes

The numbers in the text are computed by `analysis/phase4/build_report.py` from the CSVs; what is written by hand is wording, file names, line numbers and hash strings, never a result. Tags: **OBSERVED** = computed from the CSVs; **INTERPRETATION** = my reading, not tested;
**UNVERIFIED** = not backed by data on disk. Scope: h_all, bell_ghz, qft, grover on Uno R3, ESP8266, ESP32; all data are post-purge (sessions 2026-10-04 to 2026-10-31);

## 1. Summary

- **Limiting factor depends on board and circuit.** h_all, bell_ghz, qft stop at a memory wall: Uno n=8 (SRAM, `build_overflow`), ESP8266 n=13 and ESP32 n=14 (`malloc_failed`); grover stops earlier on the ESP boards by **time** (ESP8266 last run n=10, 28 min per run, 4 reps; ESP32 n=12, 75 min per run, 1 rep).
- **Memory:** floor(log2(B/8)) with B read at n=2 reproduces the observed wall on all three boards. The test resolves B only to a factor of 2, the Uno B is derived from the same compiler RAM figure that triggers `build_overflow` (close to circular), and on the ESP32 the largest free block is right while the total free heap is one qubit too high; both are reported.
- **Time model:** a per-gate-kind model (M2) predicts held-out largest-n times with MAPE 17 % over all 32 non-weak held-out points (17 % over the 30 that are also timing class A; ESP32 6 %, ESP8266 15 %, Uno 25 %); one constant per board (M1) gives 47 %. M2's 'identifiable' label is **unstable**: by the rule it holds on held-out 2: esp32, esp8266, uno; held-out 3: esp32, esp8266; held-out 4: esp32, esp8266 (the smallest coefficient of a passing fit is 0.0135 (held-out 4, ESP8266), that of a failing fit is 0.0000). The fitted constants are a fit, not measured gate costs (the ESP8266 c_CNOT implies 4.18 CPU cycles per loop iteration, which is physically impossible).
- **Offload crossover** is reported as two brackets of measured n (fast and slow PC simulation, section 5): 10 of 12 board x circuit pairs have a bracket under both bounds, 1 have no crossover up to the largest measured n under at least one bound, 10 bracket labels are *within noise* and 4 rest on a weak point. The bands reflect host PC speed (about 2x spread of the simulation time between sessions); one PC, one baud rate (115 200).
- **The grover 'infidelity 0.109 at n=12' is not a wrong state of the PC build (the MCU builds were not tested, section 8 item 2):** the float32 state keeps its direction (normalised infidelity 5.5e-05 for n <= 12; beyond n=12 the direction error also grows: n=13: 1.1e-03, n=14: 1.7e-02 (above the 1e-3 gate from n=13); double matches theory) and loses norm mainly because the float32 constant 1/sqrt(2) is biased (the constant alone over-predicts the loss by about 16 % for n = 8..13 only; at n = 14 the predicted-over-measured log loss is 1.25x, so the statement does not extend beyond n = 13, section 6). The counter's 1.71 s vs 4520 s case was a **pairing artefact, not a range limit** (section 7).

## 3. Memory limit

Model (`model.py: memory_model`): a point fits if overhead + 8 x 2^n <= B (8 bytes = complex float32); B and overhead are read from the low-n runs only. Uno: static array, B = 2048 B minus `compile_ram_used_bytes - 8*2^n` (constant per circuit, 200-206 B). ESP8266: B = `free_heap_before + 8*2^2` at n=2 (the heap is read after the allocation, `firmware/esp8266/src/main.cpp:85`, `:99`). ESP32: total free heap or `largest_block` (`heap_caps_get_largest_free_block`), one contiguous block is needed.

## 4. Time limit and prediction model

Gate counts: `firmware/common/circuits.hpp` (`analysis/phase4/gatecount.py`, cross-checked by running the unmodified header in C++ with counting kernels, 52 of 52 (circuit, n) pairs equal): h_all `:20-25`, bell_ghz `:33-39`, qft `:53-61,80-83`, grover `:152-181` with MC-phase `:108-125` and MC-X `:127-135`. Every kernel loops over all 2^n indices (`firmware/common/qsim.hpp:65,86,107`), so **work(n) = gate calls x 2^n loop iterations** (init sweep not counted).

- **M1**: t = c x work(n), one constant per board (1 parameter). **M1c**: one constant per (board, circuit). **M2**: t = (c_H N_H + c_CNOT N_CNOT + c_CPHASE N_CPHASE) 2^n + a G, non-negative least squares on relative error (4 parameters per board, shared by the circuits). **M2g**: M2 fitted without grover.
- **Split (the split and the floor were fixed before any test point was scored; the weak, hash and identifiability rules were not, see below):** per series the largest 3 ok n are held out; every other point with median >= 100 us is a fit point unless it is weak or hash-flagged (then it is not fitted either). `timemodel.assert_homogeneous` fails if a fit mixes firmware timing classes.

**Status of the choice of M2 (stated plainly).** M2 has the functional form of "Model B" of `analysis/phase2_gate_cost_fit.py` / `docs/report/paper_outline.md`, whose out-of-sample errors on grover were already known before the Phase 4 analysis was started. M2 was written down in the first Phase 4 commit (`bf9b692`) before this analysis was first run, but it was **not defined blind**: it was not chosen before test errors on grover had been seen by the project. Fixed before scoring: the split (held-out = largest 3 n) and the 100 us floor. **Not** fixed before scoring: the weak rule (the first analysis used 10 repetitions, this fix round uses 30, after the first test scores were known), the hash classes, the identifiable rule and its boundary condition, the effective-repetition check (section 2), and, set on 2026-10-31 after the data and scores were known: the **session selection** (`analysis/common/sessions.py`: ESP8266 Phase 2 grover n=2-8 and ESP8266 Phase 3 from the repeat session r2, ESP32 grover n=9-10 from r2), the **counter-session fallback** (counter pairs of ESP8266 grover n=7 from r2b and of ESP32 grover n=9 from r2c, chosen because those sessions have usable pairs, an availability-conditioned choice), the counter plausibility rule **R5**, and the **band and bracket design** of the offload crossover (section 5). Effect of the weak switch alone: M2 over all series 16.3 % with the weak points and 17.0 % without; the effect of the other rules and of the session selection together is the previous-versus-now comparison of section 11 (the ESP8266 M2 error moved), which is not split by rule. The condition-number cap (1e+04) never binds (largest value 4.7); the rule that binds is the zero-boundary condition, which has no claim of being set in advance. Held-out = largest 3 n stays primary; the sensitivity to 2 and 4 is reported in full.

## 6. Grover correctness (item 1)

**Question.** Phase 1 reports grover infidelity 0.109 and P(marked) 0.89 at n=12 against Aer. Is that rounding, a definition mismatch, or a bug?

**(a) Definitions, from code.** Firmware `firmware/common/circuits.hpp:152-181`: H on all qubits, then k = floor(pi/4 sqrt(2^n)) iterations (`:158-160`, evaluated in float32) of oracle `mc_phase_apply(n-1, n-1, pi)` (`:164`, phase flip of |1...1>, controls qubits 0..n-2, target n-1; decomposition `:108-125`, `:127-135`) and diffusion H^n, X^n (as H Z H, `:168-172`), the same multi-controlled phase (`:173`), X^n, H^n (`:174-179`). The Phase 1 and Phase 3 reference `analysis/phase1_compare.py:123-145` (`build_qiskit_circuit`) builds the same iteration count in float64 (`:129`) and the same decomposition (`_mc_phase_qiskit` `:73-89`, `_mc_x_qiskit` `:92-99`). **Tests** (`analysis/phase4/grover_test.py`): k is equal for every n = 2..22; the gate sequence (kind, qubits, angle) of the firmware circuit equals the Qiskit circuit gate by gate for all four circuits at n = 2..7 and grover n=8; marked state |1...1> in both.

## 8. Limitations

1. **Counter uncalibrated** (section 2), usable only as in section 7. T_max = 3.52e+06 s is a representation limit of the firmware (and the host also flags pulses above the armed `max_width_s`); the counter was compared with micros() up to 529 s on **one weak point** (13 pairs) and up to 0.9 s on non-weak points. The agreement is between two uncalibrated clocks.
2. **No board-level correctness data.** Boards return timing only; correctness was validated on the PC build only. Grover (section 6): the float32 state is the right state up to a norm loss (the raw Phase 1 criterion fails from n=9; direction error <= 5.5e-05 for n <= 12; beyond n=12 the direction error also grows: n=13: 1.1e-03, n=14: 1.7e-02 (above the 1e-3 gate from n=13)); whether the MCU builds behave identically is UNVERIFIED.
3. **Phase 3 samples one shot** per request on the PC (`pc/phase3_offload/sweep.py: simulate_and_sample`) and compares it with no reference; the round trip is a latency, not a correctness result.
4. **One unit per board, one PC, one baud rate (115 200).** The crossover was not tested at other baud rates.
5. **Provenance:** 70 of 70 sessions carry a dirty-tree flag, 69 have a firmware source hash equal to a committed tree and 1 ran uncommitted firmware; no session recorded a hash of the flashed binary (`docs/reproducibility/session_provenance.md`).
6. **Weak points** (fewer than 30 independent repetitions or requests; rule in `analysis/common/weak.py`): ESP32 grover n=9 (15 independent of 100 recorded rows); ESP32 grover n=10 (29 independent of 100 recorded rows); ESP32 grover n=11 (13 reps); ESP32 grover n=12 (1 reps); ESP8266 grover n=10 (4 reps); Phase 3 grover n=12, 13 on the ESP boards (2-42 requests at n >= 11). They are never fitted or scored in the primary numbers.
7. **Firmware class mixing that remains** (section 2): all selected ESP8266 grover points are class B (timer1 interrupt inside `t0..t1`) while the other ESP8266 circuits are class A, so ESP8266 grover is outside the fit and the clean scores; ESP32 grover n=2-8, 11 (hash 5f10cc9afc3fffe5) and n=9-10, 12 (hash 4478147465f99625) are both class A by reading the diff (UNVERIFIED: no binary hash was recorded).
8. The M2 constants are fitted values, not measured gate costs; M2 is not identifiable on Uno R3; M2 was not defined blind with respect to grover (section 4).
9. Uno `micros()` resolution is 4 us, ESP boards 1 us; the model ignores points below 100 us.
10. **Counter pairing fails on some long points.** Points of the selected data without a usable counter pair at n >= 7: ESP32 P2 bell_ghz n=14; ESP32 P2 grover n=12; ESP32 P2 h_all n=14; ESP32 P2 qft n=14; ESP8266 P2 bell_ghz n=9,12,13; ESP8266 P2 grover n=8,9,10; ESP8266 P2 h_all n=10,11,12,13; ESP8266 P2 qft n=9,10,11,12,13; Uno R3 P2 bell_ghz n=8; Uno R3 P2 grover n=8; Uno R3 P2 h_all n=8; Uno R3 P2 qft n=8; ESP32 P3 grover n=10,11,13; ESP8266 P3 grover n=9,10; Uno R3 P3 grover n=7,8; Uno R3 P3 h_all n=7. The ESP8266 Phase 2 grover n=8 point failed in three sessions (r2, r2b, r2c). INTERPRETATION: line noise on the counter input (the hardware PCNT, an edge counter independent of the capture interrupt, counted more edges than the capture interrupt handled in the `#D` diagnostic lines of the failed runs; short points pair 100/100); the cause is not established (UNVERIFIED: WiFi activity of the counter, power or wiring). The micros() data of these points are unaffected and used; only the micros()-vs-counter agreement lacks them.
11. **Counter wrap artefact.** `unwrap_ticks` (`firmware/counter_esp32/src/main.cpp`) turns a spike whose fall stamp precedes the rise stamp by a few ticks into 2^32 - 1 ticks = 53.687 s, which the glitch filter does not remove. The plausibility rule R5 (`analysis/common/counter_validity.py`) rejects a pair whose counter/micros() ratio is outside [0.5, 2.0] or whose counter value is within 1 ms of k x 53.687091 s; the count rule is not relaxed, so one such pair makes the point unusable. It changed these points: ESP32 bell_ghz P2 n=3 (with_ref); ESP32 h_all P2 n=2 (with_ref); Uno R3 qft P2 n=3 (with_ref); Uno R3 qft P2 n=4 (with_ref). The counter firmware was not changed.
12. **PC simulation speed differs between sessions.** The Aer simulation time of one Phase 3 circuit x n (n >= 6) differs by a median 1.53x and at most 2.92x between the fastest and the slowest session on the same PC (grover n=9: 4.1-8.8 s), while the board-and-link residual of the selected sessions has a median of 753 us (IQR 486-3214 us; at large n it also absorbs the bookkeeping error of subtracting two medians); the crossovers are therefore reported as brackets under the fastest and the slowest observed simulation (section 5). The cause of the PC speed change is not recorded in the sidecars (UNVERIFIED).
13. **Counter self-test.** The loopback self-test (GPIO25 to GPIO35, `data/calibration/counter_selftest_20261031.txt`) passed 5 of 5 scenarios on 2026-10-31; the counter reads 2.0 to 2.9 us longer than the generated width at 100 us to 10 s (a constant offset of the loopback; one crystal generates and measures, so clock accuracy and the GPIO34 target pad are not tested; the offset cannot be split between generator and counter).
